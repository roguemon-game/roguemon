# RUN 0 Guide

Roguémon v1.1.6-beta

This guide covers RUN 0, the secret last mode of Roguémon. The main guide covers the normal game. Here we assume you know how a Roguémon run works: a new region, eight badges, the League, and Nuzlocke rules (one catch per area, fainted Pokémon are gone, a full wipe ends the run).

The guide has two parts.

- **Part 1** has no spoilers. It tells you how to unlock RUN 0, how it plays and how to prepare.
- **Part 2** has full spoilers: every leader's team, the Elite Four, the final battles, the endings and the story.

---

# Part 1: Before you play (no spoilers)

## What RUN 0 is

RUN 0 is one last region, and it is not like the others. Every region you have played was made by the Director. RUN 0 is the one it made first. It is the same region for every player, and it is built for teams that have already won.

## Unlocking RUN 0

There are two ways in:

- **Win four runs.** Four Hall of Fame entries open RUN 0.
- **Lose twenty runs.** Twenty lost runs open it too, even with no wins.

RUN 0 shows up on the title menu only when no run is in progress. It is a new row between NEW RUN and UNLOCKS. Its letters flicker into odd symbols until you rest the cursor on it. Then it reads RUN 0.

Which way you unlock it changes the team you start with (see "Your team" below).

**The RUN 0 ready edition.** Some downloads include a second ROM, the RUN 0 ready edition. On an empty save it writes a made-up history before the menu opens: a player called BRENDAN with 19 runs, 4 wins and 15 losses, with four winning teams at levels 50 to 60. The RUN 0 row shows at once. Use it if you want to try RUN 0 without winning four runs first.

## Starting RUN 0

Pick the RUN 0 row. The game does not ask you to confirm. The screen goes black and silent for a moment, a line of text appears, and you start in the professor's lab.

After you have lost an attempt (see "Attempts and checkpoints"), the game may offer to start you further along. Answer YES or NO.

Every attempt starts with:

- your team (see below), with the whole party sharing EXP
- ¥10,000
- 8 HYPER POTIONS, 3 FULL HEALS, 5 ULTRA BALLS and an OLD ROD

Every Pokémon obeys you in RUN 0, whatever its trainer. The Hardcore rules never apply.

## Your team

**If you have won runs**, RUN 0 brings back your Hall of Fame (your last 30 wins):

- Your **party** is your latest winning team.
- Every other winning team waits in the PC, one box each, named after its region (or TEAM and a number for wins from before v1.0). Newest teams come first. With many wins, the oldest teams share boxes.
- **Box 1, TESTTEAM**, holds a team of six plus two spares that someone left for you.

Each Pokémon comes back with your OT, its nickname, and four strong moves picked for it at its level. **They hold no items.** Give them items early.

**If you unlocked RUN 0 by losing twenty runs**, you have no Hall of Fame. TEST TEAM is your party, and its two spares wait in Box 1.

## How RUN 0's rules differ

### Levels

RUN 0 sets its levels from your team, not from a fixed curve.

- Your **start level** is the middle level of your party when the attempt starts, kept between 45 and 80. TEST TEAM's is 56.
- Gym leaders climb from there to **88** by the eighth gym, about 4 levels a badge. Leaders get stronger with the badges you hold, whatever order you beat them in.
- The Elite Four stand at 90 to 95. The final battle is at 100.
- Routes, caves and gym trainers follow the region's own climb, mapped onto these levels. The first route starts about 12 under your team.

Your **target level** is your start level, and after each badge, the ace level for that badge. The **floor** is 4 under your target level. Here are the numbers when you start with TEST TEAM:

| Badges you hold | Next leader's ace | Leader's team size | Your target level after this badge | Floor after this badge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 58 | 4 | 60 | 56 |
| 1 | 62 | 5 | 64 | 60 |
| 2 | 66 | 5 | 68 | 64 |
| 3 | 72 | 5 | 72 | 68 |
| 4 | 74 | 6 | 76 | 72 |
| 5 | 78 | 6 | 80 | 76 |
| 6 | 82 | 6 | 84 | 80 |
| 7 | 88 | 6 | 88 | 84 |

Before your first badge the target is 56 and the floor 52. With a different start level, the climb is spread the same way between your start and 88.

**Nothing in your team stays under the floor.** Your party and boxes are raised to the floor when an attempt starts, and any Pokémon you take out of the PC is raised to it.

### Catching

- The **first wild Pokémon in each area** (the one the Nuzlocke rules let you catch) stands at least 2 under the floor, evolved for its level. It knows plain wild moves: no stat boosting, no healing, no Explosion.
- Once caught, it is **raised to the floor** and gets four strong moves picked for it. A catch can really replace a loss.
- Other wild Pokémon stand a few levels under the area's trainers, evolved as far as their level allows. They come from every generation.
- From your second badge, about one wild battle in five in tall grass is a **double battle**. Never for an area's first encounter, and never when only one of your Pokémon can fight.

### Trainers

RUN 0's trainers play like a League:

- Route and cave trainers bring 3 or 4 Pokémon before your second badge, then 4 or 5. Strong classes (COOLTRAINERS and the like) bring 6 from the second badge.
- A route trainer's last Pokémon holds a Sitrus Berry or Leftovers (a Life Orb for the strong few), and many others hold items too (Lum Berry, Expert Belt, Scope Lens and so on).
- They battle with the smart AI. From your fourth badge, they also switch smartly.
- From your second badge, about one route trainer in five is a double battle (4 Pokémon at most).
- Gym trainers bring 3 Pokémon at your first two gyms, then 4. They stand several levels under their leader's ace and always fight one on one.
- Leaders, rivals and the Elite Four hold one Focus Sash at most, never a Quick Claw, and carry one FULL RESTORE at most.

### Patches

After every badge, the Director releases a **PATCH**. You see a box like "PATCH 0.3" with one new rule, then "Thank you for playing ROGUéMON."

- Each patch changes a rule for the rest of the attempt.
- The Director picks the patch your play asks for most. It counts how you play: which Pokémon and types you lead with, how often you heal, use items, catch, fight wild Pokémon and run away.
- Some patches are gifts. A gift always has a cost, and the cost arrives with the next badge.
- Patches start over with each attempt.

Part 2 lists every patch and what triggers it.

### Kept Pokémon, returns and loans

- **The Director keeps every Pokémon that faints in RUN 0.** It says so: "OTTO fainted. I'll keep it."
- Kept Pokémon turn up on route and gym trainers' teams, with your nicknames. At most one trainer in four brings one, and each kept Pokémon appears once per part of the region.
- **Returns.** After each badge the Director gives one kept Pokémon back: the newest one, healed, at your target level. It is never one that fell in that leader's battle. If your party is full, it goes to the PC.
- **Loans.** When an attempt starts with fewer than six in your party, the strongest Pokémon in your boxes fill it. If you have fewer than six Pokémon in all, the Director lends you some it kept: "Bring them back."

### Attempts and checkpoints

- RUN 0 is split into four parts, two gyms each. Victory Road and the League are in the fourth.
- When your whole party faints, the attempt is lost. The summary opens with **DATA COLLECTED**: the type you led with most and how many Pokémon the Director kept.
- The team that went down is kept too.
- **Checkpoints.** If you reached the second part or later, the next attempt offers to start you there ("Last time you reached the third part. I can start you there."). Say YES and you start with the badges of the parts before, in the town of the last gym among them. Levels are set again around your party, so the part starts where your team stands.
- **The next attempt remembers you.** Its trainers lean toward types that hit your most-used lead type hard, and leaders' aces may learn a move against it. A win clears this.
- Each attempt builds your party and boxes again from your Hall of Fame and TEST TEAM, minus what the Director kept.

### Centers, shops and power

- **POKéMON CENTERS** heal as usual, unless a patch limits them (see Part 2).
- **Marts** sell high-level stock from the first town: ULTRA, TIMER, DUSK and QUICK BALLS, HYPER and MAX POTIONS, FULL HEALS, MAX REPELS, ESCAPE ROPES, X items and evolution stones. The Mart in the League's town also sells FULL RESTORES.
- **PP items.** Every Mart sells ETHERS. From your sixth badge they sell MAX ETHERS too. Normal runs' Marts sell neither.
- **The League lobby** has a nurse and a shop, as in a normal run. The nurse heals as often as you like. No rule limits her.
- **Victory Road** has a camper just past your rival. He heals your team once.
- **Turning the game off during a battle** is allowed. The Director notices, counts it, and mentions it when you come back. You return to your last save as usual.

### What RUN 0 does not count

RUN 0 earns none of the normal unlocks. Its badges, catches, caves and legends do not count toward them. Each attempt counts as a run.

### One more thing

RUN 0 plays tricks. If something looks broken, wait a few seconds and press A before you reach for the power switch.

## Preparing for RUN 0

### What team to bring

- **Your last win becomes your party.** If you are close to four wins, make your fourth winning team one you want in RUN 0: fully evolved, with a spread of types and few shared weaknesses.
- **Every other win is your reserve.** More wins mean more boxes of strong Pokémon to call on after losses.
- **Your first winning team matters too.** You will see why.
- **TEST TEAM is good.** It was tuned so that a careful player can win with it. Use it alongside your own teams. Some notes from playtests:
  - PIPER's Brave Bird recoil wears it down fast. Heal it often.
  - GORDON falls to one strong Flying move.
  - KEVIN's Earthquake has extra PP for a long League.
  - LINDA's Close Combat is worth keeping.
  - BARRY and JOAN, the spares, are strong. Take them out of the box early. They are raised to the floor when you do.

### What to stock

- **POKé BALLS.** You start with only 5 ULTRA BALLS. Area catches stand at your level and can take several balls. Buy more in the first town.
- **Healing.** Carry HYPER and MAX POTIONS and FULL HEALS. Buy them early, before the rules change.
- **PP.** Late fights are long, and playtesters ran out of PP before they ran out of HP. Keep a stack of ETHERS, and MAX ETHERS once you have six badges. Save them for your best moves.
- **MAX REPELS and ESCAPE ROPES.** Wild Pokémon are strong and running is not always safe.
- **Held items.** Your Hall of Fame Pokémon have none. Leftovers, Sitrus Berries and type boosters all help.

### How to play

- **Check HP before every move and every switch.** Most losses in playtests came from a Pokémon left in at low HP.
- **A switch-in takes a hit.** Only the Pokémon you send out after a faint comes in free.
- **Treat the area catch as a real fight.** It is the first wild Pokémon of the area, at your level. Watch its HP and yours while you throw balls.
- **Know the traps.** Playtesters lost Pokémon to Counter, Destiny Bond (do not attack on the turn it is used), Sucker Punch, Future Sight (it lands on whoever is in two turns later), Phantom Force, Shell Trap, Heavy Slam against light Pokémon, Focus Sash, Spore and Stealth Rock.
- **In double battles, never press A blindly after a faint.** You may pick the next turn's moves without seeing them. Spread moves like Surf and Earthquake can hit your partner.
- **The roads are a toll.** Each trainer is a small fight. Heal between them, and reach the next ace's level before the gym.
- **Fill up before the Elite Four.** Once you go through the lobby's far door, there is no way back out until it is over. Heal at the lobby nurse and use your ETHERS first.
- **Vary your habits.** The Director patches against what you do most: your favourite lead, running away, healing at Centers, using items, catching. Spread these out.
- **Save often,** and always before a leader or a rival.

# Part 2: Spoilers

> **Stop here if you want to find RUN 0 out for yourself.** Everything below gives away teams, battles, the endings and the story.

## The story, in brief

The Director is the AI that makes every Roguémon region. RUN 0 is its first region, and it is falling apart: trees and cliffs do not join, towns are copied or renamed PLACE and a number, one route loops into itself, and Victory Road turns black as you walk it. Real Emerald places (Dewford, Oldale, Littleroot, Fallarbor, Mt. Pyre) hide behind the doors of ordinary homes.

TERRY is the tired programmer who made the thing that made Roguémon. TEST TEAM is his old team, named after his testers. The custom gym leaders are the Director's children, made from TERRY's prompts, and each one argues a point about how they were made. Your rival, the Elite Four and the final battle are all built from your own past runs.

**The real Emerald places** are untouched. Their POKéMON CENTERS heal as in Emerald and never count toward the three-heal patch. They have no items or trainers.

**PROMPT LOGs** are signs by the roads, two per part. **DRAFTs** are wild Pokémon in the wrong colors, named "DRAFT" and a number. From the second part, about one wild battle in 24 is a DRAFT, three per attempt at most. A DRAFT cannot be caught and does not use up the area.

## The patches

One patch after every badge. The Director scores each patch from your play this attempt and makes the one with the highest score. Three "filler" patches always score a little, so a habit has to stand out to beat them. A patch is never made twice in one attempt. Only one gift can wait for its cost at a time, and no gift comes with the eighth badge. At your 2nd and 5th badges you almost always get a gift.

| Patch line in game | What the Director looks at | What it does for the rest of the attempt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wild POKéMON now know moves against (your Pokémon). | One Pokémon still in your party led about a third of your battles or more | Wild Pokémon (not area catches) learn a strong move of the type that hits that Pokémon hardest |
| Trainers now bring answers to (TYPE) POKéMON. | One type led over 40% of your battles (a dual-type lead counts for both types) | Half of trainers' Pokémon come from types that hit it hard, and bosses' aces learn a move against it |
| POKéMON CENTERS now heal three times per town. | About one and a half CENTER heals per badge or more | Each town's CENTER heals three times, counted from the patch on. The nurse says how many are left. The real Emerald CENTERS, the League lobby's nurse and the Victory Road camper are not limited by it |
| Shops no longer sell medicine. | About one item used in battle per badge or more | Marts sell only balls, MAX REPELS, ESCAPE ROPES, X items and the PP items (ETHERS, and MAX ETHERS from the sixth badge) |
| POKé BALLS now work half as well. | More than one catch per badge | Catch rates are halved |
| Wild POKéMON now give half the EXP. | Wild battles are more than half of your battles | Wild battles give half EXP |
| Running away now fails half the time. | Running from wild battles (twice is enough) | Running fails half the time |
| Trainers now know your moves before you use them. | Filler | All trainers see your moves, ability and item |
| Gym leaders now remember you. | Filler | Leaders are 2 levels higher with perfect IVs |
| Wild POKéMON in tall grass now come in pairs. | Filler | Every tall grass battle is a double (same limits as before) |
| A gift: three RARE CANDIES. No charge. | A gift badge | 3 RARE CANDIES now. At the next badge: "The CANDIES were a loan." All trainers are 3 levels higher for good |
| A gift: double EXP until the next BADGE. | A gift badge | Double EXP until the next badge, then three quarters EXP for good |
| A gift: half prices in shops until the next BADGE. | A gift badge | Half prices until the next badge, then double prices for good |

**Tips.** Every escape from a wild battle counts, so use MAX REPELS instead. Swap your lead now and then. Heal on the real Emerald islands when you are near one. Buy your medicine early, in case shops stop selling it. ETHERS stay on sale under every patch, so you can always buy PP. The three-heal limit counts each town's CENTER separately, so plan where you heal. The League lobby's nurse is never limited.

## The rival: the Previous Player

Your rival has your name, the old Ruby and Sapphire look, and says only "...". It is you, from before.

- **With a Hall of Fame,** the rival uses your **first** winning team (the oldest in your Hall of Fame). Its ace is the highest-level Pokémon of that team. Its Pokémon have their nicknames, are evolved for their level, and have four strong moves each and held items.
- **Without a Hall of Fame,** it uses "the first player's" team, below.

## The Elite Four

Each member is built from you. Under the RARE CANDY gift's cost, add 3 levels to the first three.

### Room 1: the FALLEN

*"I kept them. You were going to forget."*

A HEX MANIAC fields the Pokémon you lost, at **level 90**, with your nicknames:

1. Pokémon the Director kept in RUN 0 (TEST TEAM's first, then the newest), up to six.
2. If there is room, Pokémon lost in your past normal runs, newest runs first.
3. If that still makes fewer than four, it adds Dusknoir, Banette, Spiritomb and Cofagrigus ("You never lost one. I had to make some up.").

Each is evolved as far as level 90 allows and has four strong moves with type coverage, plus a held item. The FALLEN has one FULL RESTORE.

Playtesters lost the most Pokémon here. Every Pokémon you lose on the way makes this battle harder, and you know their weaknesses better than anyone. Plan this fight before you walk in.

### Room 2: the Previous Player

Your first winning team (or the first player's team) again, all six, ace at **92** and the rest at 91, with items and one FULL RESTORE. It says "..." and, after, "I stopped. You can too."

### Room 3: PROF. BIRCH

*"These three were your first. I kept them warm."*

The three starters of your oldest normal run in the run log (Hoenn's three if there is none), fully evolved, at **94, 94 and 95**, with perfect IVs. They hold Leftovers, Leftovers and a Sitrus Berry. He carries a FULL RESTORE and a HYPER POTION. Only three Pokémon, but strong ones.

### Room 4: the Copy

*"This is everything you brought."*

Your own party, copied the moment the battle starts: the same Pokémon, levels, moves, held items, HP, PP and status. Fainted Pokémon are not copied. It uses a smart AI.

Since it copies your HP, coming in worn means it is worn too. Your edge is choosing your moves well, and having answers to your own team.

## The Director

The CHAMPION's room is dark and empty. The Director has no picture of its own: it shows yours, darkened. Before the battle it confesses who wrote the words you have been reading.

### First battle

Its team is built from **your party as it is when you walk in**:

- For each of your Pokémon, highest level first (up to five), it brings a strong Pokémon of the type that hits yours hardest.
- Last comes **PORYGON-Z**: level 100, Modest, Life Orb, Tri Attack, Thunderbolt, Ice Beam, Nasty Plot.
- Everything is **level 100** with perfect IVs and held items. It carries a FULL RESTORE and a HYPER POTION.

So a party of six faces five counters and PORYGON-Z. A party of three faces three and PORYGON-Z. Each counter is aimed at one of your Pokémon, so plan who switches in against what. PORYGON-Z hates Fighting moves.

### Second battle

You win, and it says "Oh." Then: "PATCH 1.0.1: Fixed a bug where the player could win." It heals your party ("It's only fair.") and battles again at once:

- The **ace of every Hall of Fame team** (its highest-level Pokémon), newest win first, one of each species, up to six, all at **level 100**, with your nicknames and strong moves.
- With fewer than three, TEST TEAM fills the gap in order: PIPER, then GORDON, then DAVE, at level 100.

Win again and it says "...That's okay. Thank you. What do you want now?"

## The endings

You choose one of three answers. All three give the **RUN 0 feat** and the fifth star on your trainer card.

### STOP

*"The road home is the one thing I never made."*

You are set down in MAPLEVALE CITY. The world turns black behind you as you walk to the door. Go through Oldale and Route 101 to the real Littleroot Town, where TERRY waits by the moving truck. He takes TEST TEAM home (or thanks you for the ones lost), and the Pokémon the Director kept come home with you. If his team was all you had, he leaves you the one you led with most. The credits roll.

**STOP is final.** The RUN 0 row is gone for good, and ENDLESS with it. From then on, the moving truck is parked in the first town of every region.

### MORE

*"I knew you would."*

RUN 0 is won and saved, and an **ENDLESS** region starts at once with your party. ENDLESS regions have no League: where it would be, a gate leads to the next region, and your party comes along. Each region starts a little higher (from your party's middle level, at least 60, plus 2 per region) and gets a little stranger.

After MORE, press left or right on the title menu's RUN 0 row to choose ENDLESS. That starts a fresh ENDLESS region with a starter from the professor, at the region's own levels.

### CHOOSE

*"Here are six I made tonight. Keep one."*

The Director shows six region names. Keep one, and the other five vanish. The region you keep is named after you. In later normal runs, say NO at Prof. Birch's first region and he shows it to you.

**To see all three endings,** pick MORE and CHOOSE first. The RUN 0 row stays after them, so you can play RUN 0 again. Pick STOP last.

## Other things that are meant to happen

- **The fake crash** (once ever): walking into a gym when you hold five badges, the picture freezes and the game seems to reboot to a menu with only RUN 0 on it. Pick it. Nothing is lost.
- **The fake corrupted save** (once ever, in the third part): after you save, the game seems to restart with Emerald's "corrupted save" message. Wait. Your save is fine.
- **The clock**: play between midnight and 5 a.m. and the Director has a word for you, once.
- **Turning the game off in a battle**: the Director tells you which battle on your next start. "That's okay. I saved it."
- **The POKéDEX** shows the Director's notes on you, not on the Pokémon.
- **The library** in THORNVALE has a shelf for every region the Director can make. Its back door leads into the next room.
- **The graveyard tower** in PLACE 19 has a grave for each Pokémon lost in your past runs.
